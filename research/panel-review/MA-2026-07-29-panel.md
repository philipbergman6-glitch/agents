### Synthesis
Three orthogonal models independently reach neutral — but for three different reasons, and that difference is the whole story. Graham's neutral is a *price* verdict: by his asset-and-earnings yardstick the stock is overpriced, full stop. Lynch's neutral is a *scorecard* verdict: his valuation dimension scored perfectly and it was growth quality (revenue lagging EPS) and leverage that held the total under the bullish bar. Buffett's neutral is a *trust* verdict: his DCF shows the largest discount on the panel, and he explicitly declines to lean on it because of what the number rests on.

Which leads to the load-bearing caveat: **all three whales independently flagged the same market-cap provenance** — today's delayed close crossed with a shares-outstanding tag dated 2010-10-27. Buffett wants it "verified before treating the discount as real"; Lynch says "treat the valuation dimension with suspicion until that's re-fetched or verified"; Graham calls the share-count tag "old" and points the reader's eye at it. Every valuation figure above — the +288%, the −29.6%, the PEG of 0.26 — rests on that one derivation. The panel's disagreement-about-reasons is genuine; the valuation numbers it disagrees over may not be. A re-fetch with a resolved share count is the obvious next step before acting on any of the three reads.

The quality picture, by contrast, needs no such asterisk and the whales largely share it: an unbroken ten-year earnings record (Buffett's moat 5-for-10-years bar and Graham's 10-of-10 EPS stability are the same fact read twice), against real leverage (debt/equity 2.82 fails Lynch's test, debt ratio 0.87 fails Graham's, and it costs Buffett fundamentals points — three rubrics, one thin-equity balance sheet, largely the product of buybacks eating the book).
